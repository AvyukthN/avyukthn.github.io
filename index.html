Empirical alignment research.

I'm an empirical alignment researcher. Most recently I mentored a SOAR cohort at EleutherAI working on inoculation prompting, where we developed Latent Preference Shift — a heuristic that predicts a prompt's inoculation effectiveness from base-model log-probabilities, replacing a fine-tuning run per candidate with a single forward pass. Before that I was a research fellow in the SPAR Spring 2026 cohort, advised by Daniel Tan, Maxime Riché, and Victor Gillioz, working on understanding and making improvements to inoculation prompting.

Prior to engaging in AI Safety I was working in computational neuroscience at CMU @ Mendoza-Halliday Lab, working on using neuronal synchrony to uncover the neuron-level mechanisms behind the ubiquitious spectrolaminar profile of LFP power across cortical layers. Our work advanced understanding of the recurrent motifs of spike-LFP synchrony and the structure of the canonical cortical microcircuit. Our work was published at SFN 2025 where we were invited to give a nanosymposium talk.

Alignment

Cheap heuristics for inoculation prompt search

EleutherAI SOAR Fellowship & SPAR · 2026 – present · with Maxime Riché, Daniel Tan, Victor Gillioz

Inoculation prompting suppresses undesired behavioral generalization by requesting the behavior during fine-tuning and dropping the request at inference. The bottleneck is selection: candidates that read almost identically differ enormously in effect, and the only reliable test is a fine-tuning run per candidate.

  • Built Latent Preference Shift, a log-linear score computed from base-model log-probabilities over a contrastive set, which ranks candidates without per-candidate training.
  • Showed the existing behavioral-elicitation baseline loses all ordering when the base model almost always or almost never exhibits the behavior, and that the latent score stays graded there.
  • Evaluated across reward hacking, sycophancy, and trait-distillation settings; scaled the candidate pool to fifty prompts to check that rank correlation holds.
  • Leading a cohort of four researchers; the project was awarded a $6,000 BlueDot rapid grant.

Mini-project · 2025

Scaling sequential memory editing in language models

University of Pittsburgh · 2025 · with Dr. Xiang Li

Treating transformer MLPs as linear associative memories, I recomputed the key projection matrix after each sequential ROME update so that the knowledge representation subspace stays aligned in the causal layers. This keeps factual edits integrated across long sequences of interventions, where ROME and MEMIT otherwise degrade.

    Scalable Search Heuristics for Inoculation Prompting

    Avyukth R. Nilajagi, Xinyue Liu, Nikhil Maturi, Bhiman Kumar Baghel

    Under submission — AI4Good workshop, NeurIPS 2026

    Selecting an effective inoculation prompt normally costs one fine-tuning run per candidate. We introduce Latent Preference Shift, which predicts effectiveness from base-model log-probabilities over a fixed contrastive set, and show it orders candidates more faithfully than the behavioral elicitation rate — especially where elicitation saturates and carries no signal at all.
